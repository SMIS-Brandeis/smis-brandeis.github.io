Student Morality Information Service

Does the way sex is discussed feel cheap to you? Are you tired of pretending that today ’s sexual culture is normal and healthy?

Our generation has forgotten that just because you can  do something doesn ’t mean you should . And it ’s more than just the way we talk about sex. Our whole culture is based on affirmation . We have convinced ourselves that all feelings need to be validated, all identities accepted, all desires normalized.  Sex, which was once a meaningful and private expression of love in a committed relationship, has become a shameless spectacle often shared by strangers.  Our innermost thoughts and emotions have become lunchtime conversation topics, and judgment and restraint have been left behind.

In the past, most believed that nature and society were beyond the control of individuals; instead, people focused on improving themselves. Today, we spend all of our energy trying to change the world, and no time on ourselves –our character traits, our desires, our identities; all of these are assumed to be fixed and immutable. The mantras of our age are “you ’re perfect just the way you are ” and “born this way.” But no one is perfect just the way they are. All of us can and must work to improve ourselves, physically, morally, and intellectually. And “born this way?” Sexuality is one thing, but attributing our moral character entirely to genetics is not an empowering confession; it ’s an abandonment of human agency.

The fact is, there is such a thing as objective morality and objective truth. Our purpose in life is not to have as much fun as possible, regardless of consequences. We are not just atoms floating meaninglessly through a void. Certainly, joy and excitement are part of a full life, but our purpose is something much more profound. As Socrates says:

“ O my friend, why do you … care so much about laying up the greatest amount of money and honor and reputation, and so little about wisdom and truth and the greatest improvement of the soul, which you never regard or heed at all? Are you not ashamed of this?” ( Apology  29d-e)

And as the prophet Micah declares:

“He has told you, O man, what is good, and what the Lord requires of you: Only to do justice, and to love goodness, and to walk modestly with your God.” (Micah 6:8)

Louis D. Brandeis tells us that humans possess reason and that it is our responsibility to shape our lives by its light. If you are ready to be bold, then join us as we seek to become more courageous, just, temperate, and wise. Join us, and we can make Brandeis a more moral campus.
